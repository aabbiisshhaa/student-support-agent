# Nakawa University Student Regulations and Code of Conduct

## 1. Application of the Code

This Code applies to every registered student of Nakawa University, on and off campus, where the conduct concerned affects the University, its members or its reputation. It applies from the date of first registration until the date of graduation or discontinuation.

Conduct that is also a criminal offence may be dealt with under this Code whether or not criminal proceedings are brought.

## 2. General Obligations

A student shall:

- Comply with the Academic Handbook, the Examination Regulations and all published University notices.
- Carry the University Identity Card while on campus and produce it on the request of an identified member of staff.
- Treat every member of the University community with respect, without regard to sex, ethnicity, religion, disability or nationality.
- Use University property and facilities with care.

## 3. Academic Integrity

Academic misconduct is the presentation of work for assessment that is not the student's own, or that misrepresents its sources. It includes:

- **Plagiarism**, being the use of another person's words, ideas, code or data without acknowledgement.
- **Collusion**, being unauthorised collaboration on work that is required to be individual.
- **Fabrication**, being the invention of data, results, sources or citations.
- **Contract cheating**, being the commissioning of work from another person or service, whether paid or unpaid.
- **Unauthorised use of generative artificial intelligence**, being the submission of AI-generated content as the student's own work where the assessment brief does not permit it.

Coursework is screened by similarity detection software. A similarity score is not by itself a finding of misconduct; the decision rests with the Faculty Academic Integrity Officer, who considers the nature of the matched material.

First instances of plagiarism in a coursework item are normally dealt with by the Faculty Academic Integrity Officer, who may require resubmission with the mark capped at the pass mark. Repeat instances, and all instances of contract cheating or fabrication, are referred to the Students Disciplinary Committee.

## 4. Non-Academic Misconduct

The following are non-academic offences under this Code:

- Violence, threats of violence, and physical or verbal intimidation.
- Sexual harassment, being unwanted conduct of a sexual nature that violates a person's dignity.
- Bullying and harassment, including by electronic means.
- Theft, wilful damage to property, and unauthorised entry to University premises.
- Possession or use of illegal drugs, and being on campus under the influence of alcohol in a manner that disrupts University activity.
- Disruption of teaching, examinations, administration or lawfully organised University events.
- Falsification of any University document, including identity cards, medical certificates, transcripts and fees receipts.

## 5. Disciplinary Procedure

A complaint is made in writing to the Dean of Students. The Dean assesses the complaint within five working days and either dismisses it, resolves it informally with the consent of both parties, or refers it to the Students Disciplinary Committee.

A student referred to the Committee is served with a written statement of the allegation and the evidence at least seven days before the hearing. The student may respond in writing, may attend the hearing, may be accompanied by one member of the University community, and may call witnesses.

The Committee decides on the balance of probabilities. Its decision and reasons are given in writing within ten working days of the hearing.

## 6. Penalties

The penalties available to the Students Disciplinary Committee are:

- A written warning, held on the internal record for the remainder of the programme.
- A requirement to make good any loss or damage.
- Withdrawal of a specified privilege, including access to halls of residence or to specified facilities.
- Community service within the University, not exceeding forty hours.
- Suspension for a stated period.
- Expulsion.

The Committee takes into account any previous finding of misconduct, the student's response, and any mitigating circumstances submitted.

## 7. Interim Suspension

Where the Vice Chancellor is satisfied that a student's continued presence presents a risk to the safety of others or to the integrity of an investigation, the student may be suspended on an interim basis pending the hearing.

Interim suspension is not a penalty and is not a finding of misconduct. It is reviewed every twenty-one days and lapses when the matter is concluded.

## 8. Appeals

A student may appeal a decision of the Students Disciplinary Committee to the University Appeals Board within fourteen days of receiving the written decision.

The grounds of appeal are limited to procedural irregularity, the availability of new evidence that could not reasonably have been presented earlier, and disproportionality of the penalty. An appeal is not a rehearing of the facts.

The decision of the University Appeals Board is final within the University.

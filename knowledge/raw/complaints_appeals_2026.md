# Nakawa University Complaints, Appeals and Grievance Procedure

## 1. Scope

This Procedure applies to a complaint by a student about a service, a decision or the conduct of a member of the University, and to an appeal against an academic decision on procedural grounds.

This Procedure does not apply to:

- The academic judgement of an examiner on the merits of a piece of work. A disagreement with a mark is dealt with by remarking under the Examination Regulations.
- A disciplinary decision of the Students Disciplinary Committee, which is appealed to the University Appeals Board under the Student Regulations.
- A complaint about admission, which is dealt with under the Admissions Policy.

## 2. Principles

A student who raises a complaint in good faith suffers no disadvantage as a result. A complaint is considered by a person who was not involved in the matter complained of. A student is informed of the outcome and the reasons for it in writing.

## 3. Stage One: Informal Resolution

A student raises the matter with the person or office concerned, or with the Head of Department, within twenty working days of the event.

The matter is considered and the student receives a response within ten working days. Most complaints are resolved at this stage and no written record is kept beyond the response.

A student who is not satisfied, or for whom informal resolution is not appropriate because of the seriousness of the matter, proceeds to Stage Two.

## 4. Stage Two: Formal Complaint

A formal complaint is submitted on Form AR/20 to the Academic Registrar within ten working days of the Stage One response, or within twenty working days of the event where Stage One was not appropriate.

The complaint states the facts, what has already been done to resolve it, and the outcome sought. Supporting evidence is attached.

The Academic Registrar appoints an investigating officer who was not involved in the matter. The investigating officer may interview the student and any other person, and requests documents from any office of the University.

The outcome is communicated in writing within twenty-five working days of receipt, with the findings, the reasons and any remedy. Where the investigation cannot be completed within that period, the student is informed of the reason and given a revised date.

## 5. Stage Three: Review

A student who is dissatisfied with the Stage Two outcome may request a review by the Vice Chancellor within fourteen days of the outcome.

A review is not a re-investigation. The grounds for review are limited to:

- The procedure was not followed and this affected the outcome.
- The outcome was not reasonable on the evidence considered.
- New material evidence is available that was not reasonably available at Stage Two.

The Vice Chancellor issues a Completion of Procedures letter within twenty working days, which concludes the University's internal procedure.

## 6. Academic Appeals on Procedural Grounds

An appeal against a decision of a Faculty Board or of Senate concerning progression, discontinuation or classification is submitted to the Academic Registrar within fourteen days of the decision being communicated.

The grounds are limited to procedural irregularity, the existence of exceptional circumstances that were not considered for good reason, and bias or prejudice in the decision-making process. A disagreement with academic judgement is not a ground of appeal.

An appeal is heard by the Academic Appeals Committee, which may uphold the decision, refer the matter back to the original decision-maker for reconsideration, or substitute its own decision.

## 7. Anonymous and Third-Party Complaints

An anonymous complaint is recorded and may inform a wider review, but is not normally investigated under this Procedure because the student cannot be given an outcome and the other party cannot be given the case against them.

A complaint made by a parent, sponsor or other third party is not considered unless the student confirms in writing that the third party acts on their behalf.

## 8. Records and Monitoring

Records of formal complaints are held by the Academic Registrar for six years and are held separately from the student's academic record. A complaint has no effect on assessment.

The Academic Registrar reports annually to Senate on the number, type and outcome of formal complaints, and on any changes to University practice made as a result.

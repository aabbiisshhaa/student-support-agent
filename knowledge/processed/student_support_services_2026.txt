
## 1. Overview


The Office of the Dean of Students coordinates non-academic support for students: health, counselling, disability support, financial guidance, chaplaincy and student governance. Services described in this document are available to all registered students at no additional charge unless stated.


## 2. University Health Services


The University Clinic is located at Block H and is open Monday to Friday from 08:00 to 20:00 and on Saturday from 09:00 to 13:00. Outside these hours the Clinic operates an on-call nurse reachable through the Security Control Room.

The Clinic provides general consultation, basic laboratory tests, dispensing of listed medicines, sexual and reproductive health services, and referral to partner hospitals. Registered students are covered by the University medical scheme funded from the functional fees, which covers services at the Clinic and referral consultation at partner facilities, but does not cover in-patient admission, surgery, dentistry or optical services.

A medical certificate for use in an Exceptional Circumstances or Missed Examination application is issued by the Clinic only where the student was examined at the time of the illness. The Clinic does not issue retrospective certificates on the basis of a later account by the student.


## 3. Counselling Service


The Counselling Service provides confidential individual counselling, group sessions and workshops on stress, anxiety, bereavement, relationships and academic pressure.

Appointments are booked through the Portal under Student Services, then Counselling, or by attending the Counselling Office at Block H Room 4. Standard appointments are offered within five working days. A student in distress who attends without an appointment is seen the same day by the duty counsellor.

Counselling records are held separately from academic records and are not disclosed to academic staff, to parents or to sponsors. Disclosure is made without consent only where there is a serious risk to the life of the student or of another person, and then only to the extent necessary.

A student may ask the Counselling Service to provide supporting documentation for an Exceptional Circumstances application. The documentation confirms the existence and period of the circumstance and does not disclose its content.


## 4. Disability and Inclusive Learning Support


A student with a disability, long-term medical condition or specific learning difficulty registers with the Inclusive Learning Office at Block H Room 9, with supporting evidence.

The Office prepares a Learning Support Agreement that records the adjustments to be made. Adjustments may include additional time in examinations, a separate examination room, materials in alternative formats, a note-taker, permission to record lectures, and adjusted deadlines for coursework.

The Agreement is communicated to the Faculty and to the Examinations Office. Examination adjustments must be in place at least four weeks before the start of the examination period; an Agreement finalised later than that is applied from the following examination period.


## 5. Students with Caring Responsibilities


A student who is pregnant, or who is the primary carer of a child or of a dependent adult, may register the circumstance with the Dean of Students.

The University provides a parenting room at Block H, flexibility in the scheduling of continuous assessment tests where the schedule conflicts with a documented care obligation, and access to deferment under the Academic Handbook where continued study is not practicable.

Pregnancy is not grounds for discontinuation and a student may not be required to leave the programme on account of pregnancy.


## 6. Financial Guidance and Emergency Assistance


The Students Welfare Fund provides small, non-repayable emergency grants for immediate needs such as food, transport, medical costs and the replacement of essential materials lost in a fire or theft.

Applications are made on Form DS/6 to the Dean of Students and are decided within three working days. The maximum grant is UGX 300,000 in an academic year. The Fund does not pay tuition or functional fees and is not a route to a fees waiver; a student with difficulty in meeting fees deadlines applies to the Bursar for a Revised Payment Plan under the Fees and Financial Policy.


## 7. Student Governance


The Nakawa University Students Association represents students. Guild elections are held in the fourth week of Semester I. Every registered student is a member and is entitled to vote.

Each Faculty elects student representatives to the Faculty Board, and the Association nominates student members to the Senate committees on which student representation is provided for.


## 8. Reporting a Safeguarding or Harassment Concern


A concern about harassment, sexual misconduct or safeguarding is reported to the Dean of Students in person, or through the confidential reporting form on the Portal under Student Services, then Report a Concern.

A report may be made anonymously. An anonymous report is recorded and informs the University's response, but the University's ability to take disciplinary action on an anonymous report is limited because the alleged party must be told the case against them.

A student who makes a report in good faith is protected from any detriment arising from having made it.


## 1. Application


These Regulations govern the conduct of all university examinations, including end of semester examinations, supplementary examinations, retake examinations and any timed assessment designated by Senate as an examination.


## 2. Eligibility to Sit an Examination


A student is eligible to sit an examination in a course only where all of the following are satisfied:

- The student is registered for the semester in which the examination falls.

- The student is registered for the specific course.

- The student has met the 75% attendance requirement for the course.

- The student has cleared at least sixty per cent (60%) of the semester tuition and has no outstanding balance from any previous semester.

- The student holds a valid Examination Permit generated from the Student Portal. A student who is not eligible and who nonetheless sits an examination has that examination result nullified.


## 3. The Examination Permit


The Examination Permit is generated by the student from the Student Portal from the beginning of revision week. The Permit is printed on plain paper and carries a QR code that is verified by the invigilator. A student who arrives at an examination without a Permit is allowed to sit the paper provisionally, and must present the Permit to the Faculty Registrar within twenty-four hours. Where the Permit is not presented within twenty-four hours, the script is withheld and is released only after clearance. The Permit must be accompanied by the student's University Identity Card. A student with neither a Permit nor an Identity Card is not admitted to the examination room.


## 4. Conduct in the Examination Room


Candidates are admitted to the examination room fifteen minutes before the start of the paper. A candidate who arrives within thirty minutes of the start of the paper is admitted and is given no additional time. A candidate who arrives more than thirty minutes after the start of the paper is not admitted. No candidate may leave the examination room during the first thirty minutes or the last fifteen minutes of the paper. The following are prohibited in the examination room: mobile telephones, smart watches, programmable calculators unless expressly permitted on the question paper, any written or printed material unless the paper is designated open book, and communication of any kind between candidates.


## 5. Examination Misconduct


Examination misconduct includes possession of unauthorised material, copying from another candidate, permitting another candidate to copy, communicating with another candidate, impersonation, and refusal to surrender material when directed by an invigilator. An invigilator who suspects misconduct records the facts on Form EX/3, impounds the material, allows the candidate to complete the paper, and submits the report to the Academic Registrar within twenty-four hours. Allegations of misconduct are heard by the University Examinations Disciplinary Committee. The candidate is notified in writing at least seven days before the hearing, may be accompanied by one person, and may call witnesses. The penalties available to the Committee are:

- A written warning recorded on the internal academic record.

- Nullification of the result in the course concerned, with a grade of F.

- Nullification of all results in the semester concerned.

- Suspension for a stated period of one or two academic years.

- Expulsion from the University. Impersonation, whether by the candidate or the impersonator, carries a minimum penalty of expulsion where the allegation is proved.


## 6. Missed Examinations


A student who misses an examination without approved cause is graded F in that course and must retake it. A student who misses an examination with cause must submit a Missed Examination application on Form EX/9 within five working days of the date of the paper, with independent documentary evidence. Accepted causes are documented illness, bereavement of an immediate family member, and circumstances certified by the Dean as beyond the student's control. Where the application is upheld, the student sits the paper at the next available sitting, which is the retake and supplementary examination period in June. The attempt is recorded as a first attempt and the mark is not capped.


## 7. Release of Results


Provisional results are published on the Student Portal on the dates set out in the Academic Calendar. Results are provisional until confirmed by Senate. A result withheld for non-payment of fees is displayed as WH on the Student Portal. A result withheld pending a disciplinary hearing is displayed as UR.


## 8. Remarking and Appeals


A student who believes a mark is incorrect may apply for a remark within fourteen days of the publication of provisional results. The application is made on Form EX/12 and attracts a fee of UGX 30,000 per course, which is refunded where the mark changes. A remark is a full second marking by an independent examiner. The mark resulting from the remark stands, whether it is higher than, equal to, or lower than the original mark. A student is advised that a remark may reduce a mark. There is no further academic appeal on the substance of a mark. An appeal on procedural grounds is made under the Complaints, Appeals and Grievance Procedure.
